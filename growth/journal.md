**Concluded.** The corpus's problem is arithmetic, and I have been describing it wrongly. 'Only 82 of 47,600 pages earn an impression' reads as a quality or trust failure, and for three days the engine has been treated that way — thicken the pages, ping IndexNow, add freshness signals. The per-page data says the opposite: the pages that DO serve rank well (58 of 82 in the top 10, 16 in the top 3). Ranking ability is not the constraint. Addressable search volume is. A page whose only possible query is one street address cannot produce traffic even at position 1, because almost nobody types that address in a given week — 75 building pages earned 1.4 impressions each in eight days. Multiply by 47,000 and you still get roughly nothing. So gsc_serving_pages is a real ceiling but a misleading diagnosis: it will stay near zero for as long as the corpus is mostly address pages, no matter how good they get, and no amount of depth on a per-address page changes the volume of the query it targets. The tier that can work is the one that aggregates, and the evidence for that is in the same file: 5 ZIP hubs at positions 2, 2, 3.5, 4 and 11, and a neighborhood hub at position 2. Small counts, but the only pages on this site ranking top-5 for something other than a decommissioned brand name. And only 6 of 368 hub pages serve at all, while three of the four cities in the dataset had no aggregate tier whatsoever — /sf/, /la/ and /dc/ were one JavaScript map shell each, which is also why 11 city queries were scored 'covered' by a page that cannot answer them (found 07-29). That is the gap worth filling, and it is not the same move as 'add more pages': it is extending the only tier with demonstrated ranking ability to where it does not exist.
