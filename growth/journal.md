# Find A Crib — growth decision journal

Append-only. One entry per review. The ledger (`techniques.json`) holds what each
technique *is* and how it scored; this holds *why* — what was observed, what was
concluded, what changed as a result, and what to watch next.

Written by the daily 6am review. Do not rewrite past entries; if a conclusion
turns out to be wrong, say so in a new entry.

## 2026-07-26 — Correcting yesterday's review: the crons were running
*claude (setup session)*

Also fixed a deploy bug this exposed — growth_run.sh existed untracked on the droplet and blocked git pull, so the review's changes did not reach production until removed; the pull-failure fallback did its job and the build still ran on the local copy.

**Watching.** That the next review reads last_run.json and does NOT repeat the cron-failure alarm. ## 2026-07-27 — Search Console wired: the 90% goal now measures 0.0%, and indexing is the proven bottleneck
*claude (setup session)*

**Observed.** GSC live as of today. Last 7 days: 8 clicks, 27 impressions, avg position 13.7. Of ~47,600 published pages, 89 earned a single impression (0.19%). Of the 74 tracked rent-stabilization queries across NYC/SF/LA/DC, ZERO rank at all — not one in the top 10, not one in the top 100 with impressions. The only clicks the site gets are for 'jayshomefinder' / 'jays home finder' (7 of 10 in the wider window), which is the DEAD BRAND of the previous site on this domain, not rent-stabilization intent.

**Concluded.** The coverage proxy was flattering us badly: it read 89.5%, actual top-10 share is 0.0%. Coverage measures whether we wrote a page, not whether anyone finds it. The engine's founding hypothesis — indexing, not content volume — is now confirmed with a number rather than inferred: 47.6k pages, 89 serving. No amount of new content matters until that ratio moves. Also worth noting the site's entire measurable search identity is currently a decommissioned brand, so there is no organic rent-stabilization presence to build on yet — we are starting from zero, not from a low base.

**Changed.** Added growth/searchconsole.py: pulls positions for every tracked query, serving-page count, and page-2 opportunities into the ledger daily (window ends 2 days back — GSC lags, and asking for today returns a partial day that reads as a cliff). Wired into the measure step and the daily report, which now shows real share, indexing ratio and search traffic instead of the coverage proxy. Key deployed to the droplet at growth/.gsc_key.json (600, gitignored) with SC_KEY_FILE in growth.env.

**Watching.** gsc_serving_pages is now THE number to watch — if daily IndexNow and the daily sitemap are working, it should climb from 89. If it is flat in two weeks, indexing needs a different attack (internal linking depth, or the 47k pages may be judged thin/duplicate by Google). Also watch whether any tracked query ever appears: the first non-zero tracked_ranking is the first real evidence the SEO corpus can rank at all.

## 2026-07-27 — Free-account onboarding, one email design, and scheduled revisits
*claude (setup session)*

**Observed.** Supabase auth runs mailer_autoconfirm with no SMTP host, so a new account gets no confirmation and no welcome — signup is followed by silence. 17 of 20 accounts have never saved a building; week-over-week retention is 6%. Meanwhile three senders existed with two different looks: notify_saved_listings.py had a house style, the buyer sequence and the report delivery were unstyled plain text.

**Concluded.** The free-account audience is small enough that I argued against it earlier on measurement grounds, and that reasoning still holds — but 'a new user hears nothing at all' is a product defect independent of whether a drip campaign moves a metric, so it was worth fixing on those terms. The sharper risk was scope: gating only the WELCOME on account age would still have fired day-3 and day-21 at 17 cold accounts the first night, which is exactly the backfill blast the owner ruled out. A hard cutover date, not just per-step age gates, is what makes 'new signups only' actually true.

**Changed.** growth/emailkit.py (shared renderer: inline CSS since Gmail strips <style>, no images, real plain-text alternative, List-Unsubscribe) with the buyer sequence and report delivery retrofitted. growth/accounts.py + migration 0004: welcome / activate / lapsed, behaviour-gated, hard CUTOVER 2026-07-27. Separate lifecycle opt-out so stopping onboarding mail doesn't cost someone their saved-building alerts. revisit_on in the ledger (30 days), surfaced in the report and given a step in the 6am agent prompt. SECURITY: lifecycle_accounts_due() was briefly callable with the browser anon key and returned every user's email — Supabase default privileges grant EXECUTE to anon/authenticated explicitly, so 'revoke from public' removed nothing. Found by testing with the anon key rather than trusting the migration.

**Watching.** First real signup through the sequence — does welcome get opened, and does anyone come back within 7 days. The revisit dates land 2026-08-25/26: that is the first real test of whether the revisit mechanism produces a decision or just a printed line. Also whether any of the 20 pre-cutover accounts ever receives anything (they must not).
