THREE DESIGNS, THREE FILES — AND THEY ARE NOT THE SAME FILE.
They are identical by eye on purpose. Only the QR pattern differs, because
each one encodes a different address. Verified by decoding each PDF before
sending, not by looking at it:

  findacrib-card-a1-70x105mm.pdf  ->  findacrib.com/c/a1
  findacrib-card-a2-70x105mm.pdf  ->  findacrib.com/c/a2
  findacrib-card-a7-70x105mm.pdf  ->  findacrib.com/c/a7

Scan each PDF with a phone to confirm — the last two characters of the
address differ. They must be printed as three separate designs and packed
separately.

SPEC
  Stand          105*70*50mm (the smaller one)
  Printed face   70 wide x 105 tall mm, PORTRAIT

  Printing       FULL-COLOUR UV PRINT — not laser engraving

  Packing        each design bagged and labelled separately

THE QR IS THE WHOLE PRODUCT
  None of the samples in the supplier video carry a QR code — every piece
  shown is NFC-only. So there is no evidence yet that this printing can
  reproduce a scannable code, and on the 105mm printed sample the small
  text under "Tap your phone" came out as a smear.

  The code here is 41mm across 33 modules, about 1.24mm per module. It has
  to print cleanly enough to scan from a phone at arm's length. That is the
  one thing the sample exists to prove.

THE ONE THING THAT MUST NOT CHANGE
  The QR is dark on light and sits on the white half of the card. A code
  printed light-on-dark is an inverted code: recent iPhones read them,
  older Android cameras do not. Do not "improve" the contrast by moving
  the code onto the blue, and do not shrink it.

WHAT EACH DESIGN IS FOR (our record, not the supplier's concern)
  a1  Laundromats
  a2  Bodegas, delis and corner stores
  a7  Public library branches and community centres

NO TAP AFFORDANCE ON THIS SIZE
  At 70mm wide there is no room for the "Tap here | Scan code" split
  without dropping the code to 0.95mm per module, which is the size range
  this supplier's printing already failed at. The whole width goes to the
  code instead. The chip still works for anyone who taps; nobody is told
  to, which is the cheaper of the two losses.
