Put a rent-stabilization checker on your site

If you run a tenant organisation, a legal-aid site, or a housing desk, you probably answer “is this building rent-stabilized?” over and over. This does it for you, on your own page. It’s free, there’s no API key, and it doesn’t track anyone.

Rent-stabilization lookup by Find A Crib — NY State DHCR registrations and NYC HPD open data.

Add it in one line

Paste this wherever you want the checker to appear:

<div id="findacrib-check">
  <p>Rent-stabilization lookup by
     <a href="https://findacrib.com/" rel="nofollow">Find A Crib</a>
     &mdash; NY State DHCR registrations and NYC HPD open data.</p>
</div>
<script src="https://findacrib.com/embed/widget.js" async></script>

The credit line inside the div is the only part you might be tempted to delete, and it is the one part doing a job when things go wrong. Once the widget loads it disappears — the checker renders in a shadow root, which stops that line from being drawn — so your readers never see both. If the script is blocked, slow, or we have an outage, that line is what your page shows instead of an empty box, and it tells a reader where the data would have come from. It is also the whole of what we ask for in return.

Need more than one on a page, or a different container? Put data-findacrib-check on any element instead of using the id.

What it checks against

  • 47,000+ NYC buildings registered rent-stabilized with NY State DHCR (2024 building files), across all five boroughs.
  • Unit count, year built, neighborhood, and open HPD violations for each match.
  • Links through to the full record on the map — owner, managing agent, and violation history.
It states its own limits, and so should you. Registration is at the building level. It does not guarantee that a specific apartment is stabilized — individual units can be deregulated, and only the DHCR rent history for that unit settles it. The widget says this on every result, because a tool that implied otherwise would be worse than no tool.

The fine print, briefly

  • No key, no signup. The lookup endpoint is keyless and rate limited per visitor IP.
  • No tracking. No cookies, no analytics, no pixel. We don’t see who your readers are.
  • No dependencies. Plain JavaScript, ~6 KB, renders in a Shadow DOM so your CSS can’t break it and its CSS can’t leak into your page.
  • Attribution, and it is deliberately nofollowed. The snippet carries a one-line credit, and the widget repeats it in its own footer. Every link either one puts on your page has rel="nofollow", and the anchor text is our name rather than a keyword phrase. That is on purpose: Google’s spam policies treat keyword-rich links embedded in widgets distributed across many sites as link spam and exempt links marked nofollow or sponsored, it asked for widget links to be nofollowed back in 2016, and it has said it may recognise a widget and discount those links automatically anyway. So a credit that passed ranking credit would be a risk to your site and worth nothing to ours. We want readers who click it, not a link that votes.
  • Bulk data. If you need the whole dataset rather than per-address lookups, that’s the Developer API.

Using it in a story?

If you’re a reporter working on rent regulation, we’re happy to pull custom cuts of the data — by neighborhood, by owner portfolio, by violation history — and we won’t ask for anything in return beyond a source credit. Email api@findacrib.com.
